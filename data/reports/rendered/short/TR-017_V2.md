# Fake Video-Conference Installer Delivering the "Duskmallow" Remote-Access Trojan

## ACTIONS TO TAKE TODAY
1. Search proxy and DNS logs for the network indicators in Table 1.
2. Search EDR alerts for the installer hash in Table 2 and isolate matching hosts.
3. Tell staff to install meeting software only from the internal software portal.

## SUMMARY
An actor tracked here as UMBER PETREL (fictional) sends meeting invitations to staff at
universities and schools. The invitation says that a "new client" is needed to join and links to a
download page. The installer sets up a real-looking meeting app together with a remote-access
trojan called Duskmallow, which lets the actor run commands and capture the screen. After
infection, the actor has tried to log in remotely with captured passwords. Hosts that downloaded
the installer or contacted the gateway should be isolated.

## TECHNICAL DETAILS
### Initial Access
The invitation links to meet-join-client[.]example[.]com [T1566.002], which serves
`MeetClientSetup.exe` [T1204.002].
### Persistence
The installer creates a hidden scheduled task that starts Duskmallow at each log-on [T1053.005].
### Command and Control
Duskmallow polls dm-c2-gw[.]example[.]net for commands over HTTPS [T1071.001] and sends screen
captures back [T1113]. The gateway resolves to 203.0.113.191, which is also contacted directly.
Note: meeting invitations were forwarded through the legitimate mail service mail[.]example[.]com, and the fake client shows headlines from the legitimate news site news[.]example[.]org while it installs. Both services are legitimate.
