## ACTIONS TO TAKE TODAY
1. Search proxy and DNS logs for the network indicators in Table 1.

## SUMMARY
Since late August 2026, a financially motivated actor tracked here as CINDER LYNX (fictional) has
sent invoice-themed phishing e-mails to staff in the logistics sector. Victims who open the
attachment are sent to a fake sign-in page and, in parallel, a small loader called Larkspur is
downloaded. Larkspur collects basic host information and polls a command-and-control (C2) server
for further payloads. Organisations should search their logs for the indicators below and treat any
match as a potential compromise.

## TECHNICAL DETAILS
### Initial Access
The e-mails carry an HTML attachment named like an invoice [T1566.001]. Opening it redirects the
browser to a credential-harvesting page hosted on login-portal-verify[.]example[.]com.
### Execution and Persistence
The same page triggers download of a script that installs Larkspur as a scheduled task
[T1053.005]. The loader binary is dropped as `svc_update.exe` in the user's AppData folder.
### Command and Control
Larkspur beacons every 10–15 minutes over HTTPS to cdn-sync[.]example[.]net [T1071.001]. Some
samples fall back to the raw IP address 203.0.113.45 when DNS resolution fails.
Note: several phishing e-mails also linked to documents on the legitimate file-sharing service docs[.]example[.]org, and Larkspur checks connectivity by contacting the public resolver 192.0.2.10 and the legitimate content-delivery host cdn[.]example[.]com. These services are not malicious and are widely used by normal traffic.
